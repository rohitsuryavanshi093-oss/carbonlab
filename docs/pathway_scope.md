CarbonLab — Initial CDR Pathway Scope

Purpose

This document defines the initial carbon-dioxide-removal (CDR) pathways considered by CarbonLab.

The initial scope is intentionally limited. CarbonLab will begin with a manageable set of pathways that represent substantially different removal mechanisms, resource requirements, storage characteristics, and technology maturity.

The scope may be revised after the evidence audit.

---

1. Initial Core Pathways

CarbonLab v0.1 will initially investigate six pathway families:

1. Afforestation / Reforestation (A/R)
2. Soil Carbon Sequestration (SCS)
3. Biochar
4. Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and Storage (BECCS)
5. Direct Air Carbon Capture and Storage (DACCS)
6. Enhanced Weathering (EW)

These pathways represent different approaches to atmospheric CO₂ removal and provide useful diversity for subsequent cost, resource, uncertainty, scenario, and optimization analysis.

---

2. Why These Six?

2.1 Afforestation / Reforestation

Represents land-based biological carbon removal through increased carbon storage in vegetation and associated terrestrial carbon pools.

Important dimensions:

- Land requirement
- Carbon storage
- Permanence/reversal risk
- Ecosystem effects
- Deployment potential

---

2.2 Soil Carbon Sequestration

Represents land-management approaches intended to increase soil carbon stocks.

Important dimensions:

- Agricultural land
- Soil carbon persistence
- Measurement uncertainty
- Management practices
- Geographic variation
- Potential co-benefits and trade-offs

---

2.3 Biochar

Represents conversion of biomass into a more persistent carbon-rich material that can be stored or applied to soils.

Important dimensions:

- Biomass availability
- Energy requirements
- Conversion efficiency
- Carbon stability
- Transport
- Feedstock competition
- Soil effects

---

2.4 Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and Storage (BECCS)

Represents systems where biomass is used for energy and biogenic CO₂ is captured and stored.

Important dimensions:

- Biomass availability
- Land
- Energy
- Capture efficiency
- Transport
- Geological storage
- Supply-chain emissions

---

2.5 Direct Air Carbon Capture and Storage (DACCS)

Represents engineered systems that directly capture CO₂ from ambient air and permanently store it, generally through geological storage.

Important dimensions:

- Electricity
- Heat
- Capture efficiency
- Capital and operating costs
- Storage
- Infrastructure
- Energy-source assumptions

---

2.6 Enhanced Weathering

Represents accelerated mineral weathering intended to increase CO₂ removal through naturally occurring geochemical processes.

Important dimensions:

- Mineral availability
- Mining
- Crushing/grinding energy
- Transport
- Reaction rates
- Land or ocean application
- Potential environmental effects

---

3. Expansion Candidates

The following pathways are not rejected. They are reserved for later investigation:

- Ocean Alkalinity Enhancement
- Coastal/Blue Carbon Restoration
- Peatland/Wetland Restoration
- Ocean Fertilization
- Other scientifically documented CDR approaches

The expansion list may change after the evidence audit.

---

4. Inclusion Criteria

A pathway may enter the quantitative CarbonLab dataset only if it satisfies the following criteria.

Criterion 1 — Genuine CDR

The activity must involve anthropogenic removal of atmospheric CO₂ and durable storage.

Fossil-fuel CCS by itself is not treated as CDR.

Criterion 2 — Sufficient Evidence

There must be sufficient credible evidence to characterize the pathway quantitatively.

Criterion 3 — Defined System Boundary

The analysis must clearly define what processes and emissions are included.

Criterion 4 — Comparable Units

Important quantities must have clearly defined units and definitions.

Criterion 5 — Uncertainty Can Be Represented

Important uncertain parameters should be representable using documented ranges or distributions where evidence permits.

Criterion 6 — Research Value

The pathway should introduce meaningful differences in the computational comparison.

---

5. Important Scientific Rule

CarbonLab will not assume that values reported by different studies are directly comparable.

For example, two studies may report a cost in USD per tonne of CO₂ removed while using different:

- system boundaries
- years
- geographic assumptions
- energy prices
- technology assumptions
- carbon accounting methods
- financing assumptions

Therefore, the evidence audit must occur before constructing comparative models.

---

6. Scope Review

This scope is provisional.

The final quantitative pathway set will be determined after:

1. Literature reconnaissance
2. Dataset reconnaissance
3. Evidence-quality assessment
4. Definition harmonization
5. Data availability assessment

A pathway may be removed, modified, or added if the evidence supports doing so.
